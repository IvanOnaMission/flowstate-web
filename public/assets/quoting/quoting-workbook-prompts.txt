# Quoting workbook: copyable prompts

Upload the full workbook and use prompt 00 for a guided walkthrough, OR use the individual prompts as you follow the lessons. You do not need to do both. Replace bracketed text with your details. Keep My Quoting Guide available in each chat. Prompt 03A is optional; prompt 09 is for everyday use after testing.

## 00 - Upload the workbook and start together

Read this entire workbook first. Tell me if any part is unreadable. Help me build a simple quoting operating system for my electrical business: one saved My Quoting Guide, organised job files and a process I have tested.

Turn the lessons into a short checklist. Work on one small step at a time, ask one clear question, and wait for my answer. Use my examples and confirmed answers before asking for more. Draft the documents for me; do not make me repeat interviews or fill blank worksheets.

Check what files you can read, create and save. Choose one Quoting folder with me. Keep progress, file locations, approvals and gaps in Start here. Distinguish "saved" from "prepared for you to save". If needed, give me an updated file or text to save, and ask me to reattach it in a new chat.

Follow every lesson, but skip optional pricing research unless I need it. Keep this focused on quoting. Do not invent job facts, costs, hours or rules. I approve labour, pricing and the finished quote; do not send quotes or order materials. Start with the first setup question.

## 01 - Capture my quoting method

Read any existing My Quoting Guide and my examples first. Confirm the job type and saved location only if missing. Walk through one real quote from enquiry to finished document across four areas: job information; materials and suppliers; employee time and pricing; and the finished quote.

Draft from the records, then ask one question at a time about gaps and decisions: what I checked, what I chose, why and from which source. If I say "it depends" or "from experience", ask what changes my decision and for a contrasting job.

Capture actual sources, product-selection rules, time allowances by role, charging rules and quote requirements. Ask for exact values or documents where needed. Separate what one example shows from a reusable rule; ask me to confirm when the rule applies and its exceptions. Never invent an answer or repeat a settled question.

Before finishing each area, explain how you would use my answers on another job. Let me correct you. Save approved instructions and linked examples in My Quoting Guide; list unresolved gaps and the next step in Start here. Confirm the saved location. Do not create or send a quote during this interview.

Save these working instructions too: one step and question at a time; reuse answers; update progress and file locations. If direct saving is unavailable, provide a file or text for me to save. Distinguish "saved" from "prepared for you to save". In new chats, request the latest guide and needed files unless you can open them.

## 02 - Draft the reusable instructions

Read My Quoting Guide and linked examples. Draft the checklist, supplier and finished-quote instructions from confirmed answers. Ask only about gaps or conflicts; do not restart the interview.

For each input, record the source, who confirms it and when it is needed: sourcing, pricing or the finished draft. Cover scope, quantities, specifications, site details, labour, costs, tax and exclusions. Pause affected work for essential gaps; separate estimates from facts.

Save supplier sources, trade/public price distinctions, product matching, packs, delivery, availability and price validity. Confirm alternative-product rules. Use my good quote for layout, wording, grouping and internal-only information. After approval, update the guide and progress; confirm the saved location.

## 03 - Save my existing pricing rules

Read the method and pricing answers in My Quoting Guide first. Draft the pricing section from my confirmed answers and records. Ask only about gaps or contradictions in materials pricing, employee time, charge-out rates, other costs, tax, rounding and exceptions.

Check against quotes I trust. Distinguish employee costs from charge-out rates and purchase costs from customer prices. Record who approves hours by role and what the customer sees. Never invent allowances or assume an old quote proves a profitable rule.

Let me approve the rules, then save them in My Quoting Guide, replacing superseded instructions. Keep unresolved decisions visible and pause the affected pricing. If my rules are clear, skip the optional research page. If not, ask whether I want help developing them. Confirm the saved location or give me the text to save.

## 03A - Optional: develop unresolved pricing rules

This is optional. Read My Quoting Guide first. If my pricing rules are already clear and approved, skip this task. Otherwise, confirm which rules need work and help me build a pricing plan for my business.

Use my quotes, job types, location, employee roles, actual costs and time, overheads and goals. Ask one question at a time for missing context. Take one unresolved pricing decision at a time and research what it needs, including useful YouTube material. Cite sources and compare approaches. Suggest deeper research only when the decision needs it.

Be honest about whether you can access videos or transcripts. Ask me to provide them if needed; do not claim to have watched material you could not access. Do not prescribe a universal margin or copy another business's rates.

Work through the decisions with me without rushing. Save proposed rules separately from approved rules within My Quoting Guide. Compare proposals against real jobs; apply changes only after I approve them. Do not change or send customer quotes.

Save results and manual steps in the guide. In a new chat, ask me to attach the latest guide unless you can open it. Do not change live jobs or send anything.

## 06 - Source materials and prepare labour allowances

Read My Quoting Guide and the confirmed brief for [past job]. Source materials using the saved rules. Show products, codes, units/packs, quantities, purchase costs, tax, delivery, availability, price dates and expiry. Use authorised sources or request a supplier quote/export. Flag unknowns and alternatives; do not purchase.

Break the work into tasks and roles. Show only time allowances supported by my guide or records, with their basis. Ask for missing hours and include preparation, travel and other charged time where required. I approve hours by role before pricing.

Use historical costs where available; label any current-price differences. After review, save the sourcing list and approved hours in the job folder. Update the guide's progress and file locations. Preserve the original job and quote.
